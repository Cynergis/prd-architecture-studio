# Getting started — from an idea to readers

Everything happens in chat with Atlas; the engine runs underneath. You type no `oto` command
unless you want to.

## 1. "Atlas, lead this product."
Atlas asks two things: **which kind of product** (the packs the marketplace offers: a report
product, an API, …) and **what you have** (nothing, notes, a brief). It makes the project and
installs the packs the type needs.

## 2. Specify
Atlas captures the specification section by section. Each section is a question someone asks
("the data team asks: where must the data come from?"); you answer, Atlas drafts, you refine
([A] elicit, [P] other perspectives) and confirm ([C]). On every confirmation the section enters
the knowledge base through the gates, and Atlas reports: "the graph answers 9 of the 14 questions
a report product must answer; these 5 are open".

If you have a brief or notes, say **"structure these notes"**: Atlas reads them in full, drafts
each section from what they support, and asks only about the gaps.

## 3. "What must this product know?"
Atlas hands the specification to OTO: the domain questions are derived from your obligations,
you confirm them, and the interview turns them into the domain vocabulary — with its reasons,
its shapes and its questions that run.

## 4. Design and flow
The architecture and the build flow, captured the same way; every component traces to a
requirement.

## 5. Build
An implementing agent asks the graph what it must know before it acts, and is BLOCKED by name
when a fact is missing.

## 6. "Publish."
The domain pack goes to the marketplace, the store is published for readers, the site is
regenerated. Atlas gives you the install line to send to a colleague.

## 7. Someone else uses it
`/plugin install report@cynergis`, then "which fields of the fund profile are still unmapped?" —
the rows, with their sources. A correction they make goes through the gates and comes back to
your project as a pull request.
